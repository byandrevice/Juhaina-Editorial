# Homepage Composition Audit

Date: 2026-08-06

Evidence: [01-homepage-mockup.png](01-homepage-mockup.png)

## Scope

Single-screen audit of the current Second Viewing homepage mockup, focusing on
composition, hierarchy, typography, color, 3D-asset treatment, background, and
navigation clarity. The screenshot still uses a vending machine for Lists and a
CRT for Projections; the approved production mapping now uses the cigarette box
for Projections and the air conditioner for Lists.

## Overall verdict

The direction is distinctive and worth preserving, but the composition is not
yet controlled. It currently reads as four separately styled objects placed on
top of an active background rather than one intentional spatial composition.
The background pulls the eye down and left, while the object cluster pulls it
up and right. Typography adds four more competing focal points instead of
binding the objects into one interface.

## Step 1 — Homepage section selection

General health: visually promising, compositionally unstable.

### Strengths

- The dark dither field creates an immediate identity and avoids a generic
  portfolio-grid appearance.
- The objects are recognizable without cards or explanatory copy.
- The irregular placement supports the desired exploratory, early-web feeling.
- Object-plus-label grouping makes the basic navigation concept understandable.

### Structural risks

1. **Two competing centers of gravity.** The strongest blue and pink energy is
   concentrated on the left and along the bottom, while three of the four
   object groups occupy the upper-middle and right. The eye is pulled toward
   background areas that contain no navigation.
2. **The layout resembles a loose 2x2 grid without receiving the benefits of a
   grid.** Lists, Moodboards, Films, and Projections form four islands with
   similar separations, but they do not create a clear orbit, diagonal, or
   reading sequence. The result feels accidentally scattered rather than
   deliberately free-form.
3. **Large unused lower space.** The navigation ends around three quarters of
   the way down the screen, while high-contrast background marks continue to
   the bottom. This makes the interface feel top-heavy even though the
   background itself feels bottom-heavy.
4. **Missing site anchor.** There is no visible Second Viewing identity or
   stable navigation anchor. The screen reads more like a menu inside another
   experience than the actual homepage of the editorial site.

### Asset risks

1. **Optical size is inconsistent.** The VCR is wide but thin and nearly merges
   with the background. The beige CRT has the largest solid visual mass. The red
   machine has the strongest color, and the flower has the strongest light-dark
   contrast. Similar bounding-box sizes do not produce similar perceived size.
2. **Camera angles do not belong to one world.** The vending machine is nearly
   front-on, the VCR and CRT use three-quarter views, and the flower is closer to
   a top-down presentation.
3. **Rendering styles vary.** The dark realistic VCR, flat colorful machine,
   clean beige CRT, and stylized flower feel sourced independently. The final
   photogrammetry flower will intensify this unless lighting and presentation
   are deliberately normalized.
4. **Labels have inconsistent object relationships.** Gaps, label widths, line
   breaks, and type scale vary enough that some labels read as captions and
   others as separate graphic elements.

### Typography and color risks

1. **Four display voices compete.** Films and Lists share one style,
   Moodboards uses another, and Projections uses a third. The variation feels
   assigned per word rather than generated by a coherent typographic system.
2. **Unequal label hierarchy.** Films and Lists are prominent; Moodboards and
   Projections appear secondary despite all four destinations having equal
   importance.
3. **Pure white is visually harsh against the saturated blue and black.** It
   increases the cut-and-paste effect and competes with the flower and beige
   object. A slightly warm neutral would integrate the text without weakening
   contrast.
4. **Background detail crosses the text field.** Fine grid texture and bright
   blue clusters create unstable local contrast, especially once type and
   objects move responsively.

## Recommended composition

Preserve the free-form idea but place it on an invisible four-anchor system.
For a desktop canvas around 1144 by 816 pixels:

- Place a small Second Viewing wordmark at approximately 5% from the left and
  5% from the top.
- Films/VCR: lower-left, centered around 23% x 58%.
- Projections/Cigarette Box: upper-middle, centered around 47% x 24%.
- Moodboards/Flower: upper-right, centered around 81% x 31%.
- Lists/Air Conditioner: lower-right, centered around 68% x 66%.

This produces a descending visual rhythm rather than an incomplete 2x2 grid.
Keep positions responsive with bounded percentage anchors rather than absolute
desktop pixels.

## Recommended asset treatment

- Normalize **optical area**, not object width. At this canvas size, aim for a
  roughly 210–260 pixel visual footprint per object.
- Enlarge the dark, slender VCR by approximately 15–20% relative to the flower.
- Give all objects a related three-quarter viewing angle and similar camera
  elevation.
- Use the same neutral key light and slightly blue ambient fill for all models.
- Avoid added cards, glow blobs, decorative rings, and individual object
  shadows. Let the objects remain free-floating.
- Use one slow rotation language: approximately 18–24 seconds per revolution,
  with varied starting phases rather than visibly different speeds.
- On hover or keyboard focus, slow or pause the rotation and clarify the label;
  do not add bouncing or zooming.

## Recommended typography

- Use no more than two type families: one expressive display system for all
  four section names and one restrained face for the site identity.
- Individual words can still feel different through width, tracking, stacking,
  or alternates within the same display system.
- Match perceived cap height across labels. At this canvas width, target about
  34–40 pixels for the short names and 30–34 pixels for the long names.
- Keep the label 16–20 pixels below its object and center the complete
  object-plus-label group together.
- Set labels in a warm off-white near `#E9E5DB`, not pure white. Use object color
  only as a restrained hover or focus accent.
- Use the exact destination name `Moodboards`; if visually stacked, treat the
  line break as styling rather than changing it to two semantic words.

## Recommended background treatment

- Keep the coarse dither concept, but remove the competing fine crosshatch
  frequency. One dominant dither scale will read more intentionally.
- Reduce the background's overall brightness by roughly 20–30% and slightly
  desaturate the brightest blue areas.
- Remove or suppress the pink-white hotspots along the bottom edge; they pull
  attention away from navigation.
- Preserve asymmetry, but shape quiet dark regions behind every label. Do this
  in the background artwork rather than adding visible boxes behind text.
- Keep the background static when reduced motion is requested.

## Interaction and accessibility risks

- The screenshot cannot confirm keyboard focus, hit areas, semantic links,
  loading states, or reduced-motion behavior.
- The entire object-plus-label group should be one link with a generous hit
  area and a visible keyboard-focus treatment.
- Section labels must remain available before the GLB files load and if WebGL
  fails.
- Continuous rotation should stop or become static under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Contrast must be checked against the final background at each responsive
  position; a single global contrast measurement will not cover this textured
  field.

## Priority order

1. Recompose the four anchors and add the site identity.
2. Replace the mockup objects with the approved VCR, flower, cigarette box, and
   air conditioner before fine-tuning sizes.
3. Normalize optical mass, camera angles, and lighting.
4. Consolidate the label typography and spacing.
5. Quiet and rebalance the background.
6. Define hover, focus, loading, failure, and reduced-motion states.
